# ForgeFlow Online Signing Workflow

Current governing-law baseline: **laws of the Islamic Republic of Iran**.

Recommended sequence for each new private-Core developer:

1. Collect only the minimum onboarding information needed for the engagement.
2. Complete the Developer/IP/Confidentiality Agreement and confirm the relationship classification under Iranian law.
3. Complete the Pre-Existing IP disclosure and any approved third-party exceptions.
4. Send the documents through the approved e-signature provider.
5. Prefer a signing process that supports the Iranian Electronic Commerce Law's stronger evidential model: signer identification, proof of intent/control, tamper detection, retained date/time and an audit trail.
6. Save the final signed documents and signature audit trail in the private legal/document system, not GitHub.
7. Record agreement version/date and access authorization.
8. Grant the minimum GitHub/system permissions needed, normally GitHub **Write** for Core development.
9. Require branch + PR + CI + CODEOWNER review for changes to `main`.
10. Review access whenever role/scope changes.
11. At termination/end of access, revoke permissions, rotate affected credentials, and complete the Offboarding Return/Deletion Certificate.

## Iran legal notes

- Electronic Commerce Law Article 7 recognizes an electronic signature where law requires a signature.
- Articles 10-16 provide requirements and stronger evidential treatment for secure electronic signatures/records.
- Keep the signed data/document accessible for later reference together with identity, origin/destination and date/time evidence where available.
- If the developer is actually an employee/worker, the IP agreement supplements rather than replaces mandatory Iranian Labour Law requirements.

See `iran-legal-basis.md` and `legal-onboarding-checklist.md`.

## Future incorporation

When a ForgeFlow company is incorporated, execute the founder-to-company IP transfer, update future agreements to name the company as the recipient, and preserve the historical signed agreements as chain-of-title evidence.
